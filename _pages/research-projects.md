<h3><a href="/research-projects/zeolite-cellulose/" style="color: #52adc8; text-decoration: none;">Natural Zeolite and Cellulose Fiber Stabilization of Sunamganj Embankment Soil</a></h3>
<p><strong>Status:</strong> Ongoing, funded by CRISP &nbsp;|&nbsp; <strong>Role:</strong> Principal Investigator &nbsp;|&nbsp; <strong>Duration:</strong> July 2026 – June 2027</p>
<p>A two-stage stabilization study on the flood-control embankments around the haor wetlands of Sunamganj — natural zeolite first (5–20%, cured), then cellulose fiber (0.25–1.0%, compacted), as a lower-carbon alternative to cement and lime. A drone-based DEM survey picks the sampling sites; direct shear, UCS and SEM-EDS find the optimum mix; PLAXIS 2D checks the factor of safety of the treated embankment; and a life cycle assessment compares it against a conventional stabilizer. I'm the Principal Investigator, supervising two B.Sc. students on the testing and modelling.</p>
<p><strong>Key areas:</strong> Natural zeolite, cellulose fiber, two-stage stabilization, drone survey, PLAXIS 2D, life cycle assessment</p>

<h3><a href="/research-projects/desiccation-ml/" style="color: #52adc8; text-decoration: none;">Gradient-Boosted and Symbolic Regression Modelling of Desiccation Cracking</a></h3>
<p><strong>Status:</strong> Ongoing &nbsp;|&nbsp; <strong>Duration:</strong> June 2026 – Present</p>
<p>The experimental desiccation work told me how much a given additive suppresses cracking, but not which variables are actually doing the work. Here I trained LightGBM, XGBoost and CatBoost, with native categorical handling, on a dataset compiled from published desiccation studies, then fitted closed-form equations to the model response by genetic-programming symbolic regression, encoding the categorical inputs through partial-dependence factors. Each crack measure turns out to be governed by a different driver.</p>
<p><strong>Key areas:</strong> Gradient boosting, symbolic regression, SHAP, desiccation cracking, embankment soil</p>

<h3><a href="/research-projects/slope-stability/" style="color: #52adc8; text-decoration: none;">Physically Admissible Slope-Stability Prediction Using Bayesian Physics-Informed Neural Networks</a></h3>
<p><strong>Status:</strong> Manuscript in preparation &nbsp;|&nbsp; <strong>Duration:</strong> December 2025 – August 2026</p>
<p>A Bayesian physics-informed network with hard monotonicity constraints on six governing parameters, pretrained on 2,000 of 3,000 automated PLAXIS 2D strength-reduction runs and fine-tuned on 275 audited field case histories. Zero monotonicity violations, while conventional baselines violate in 19–31% of out-of-range sweeps. Pretraining improved cross-validated accuracy in all five folds.</p>
<p><strong>Key areas:</strong> Physics-informed neural networks, Bayesian deep learning, slope stability, geotechnical classification</p>

<h3><a href="/research-projects/bio-cementation/" style="color: #52adc8; text-decoration: none;">Assessment of Bio-cementation Capability of <em>Arthrospira platensis</em> in Sand Solidification</a></h3>
<p><strong>Status:</strong> First-author manuscript under review at <em>Geosystems and Geoenvironment</em> &nbsp;|&nbsp; <strong>Duration:</strong> 2023 – 2024</p>
<p>My undergraduate thesis. Ottawa sand treated by surface percolation with equimolar CaCl₂ and urea at five concentrations, measuring permeability, direct shear and ultrasonic pulse velocity. Permeability fell 71.9% at 1.00 M and shear strength peaked at 0.60 M. SEM-EDS confirmed the calcite bonding mechanism.</p>
<p><strong>Key areas:</strong> Bio-cementation, cyanobacteria-induced calcite precipitation, sustainable soil stabilization, MICP</p>

<h3><a href="/research-projects/desiccation-cracking/" style="color: #52adc8; text-decoration: none;">Mitigating Desiccation-Induced Cracking of Embankment Soil Using Fiber Reinforcement and Fly Ash</a></h3>
<p><strong>Status:</strong> Published in <em>Geotechnical and Geological Engineering</em> (2025) &nbsp;|&nbsp; <strong>Duration:</strong> 2023 – 2024</p>
<p>Two fibers were tested — polypropylene first and then polyester — with and without fly ash on desiccation-prone embankment soil. The combined treatment of polyester fiber and fly ash achieved up to 96.4% reduction in surface crack ratio and 45% increase in unconfined compressive strength.</p>
<p><strong>Key areas:</strong> Desiccation cracking, fiber reinforcement, fly ash stabilization, embankment soil, crack mitigation</p>

<h3><a href="/research-projects/embankment-soil/" style="color: #52adc8; text-decoration: none;">Remediation of Desiccation-Ridden Embankment Soil Using Waste Plastic Fiber, Fly Ash, and Lime</a></h3>
<p><strong>Status:</strong> Under review at <em>Indian Geotechnical Journal</em> &nbsp;|&nbsp; <strong>Duration:</strong> 2022 – 2024</p>
<p>A three-stage additive optimisation strategy — waste plastic fiber (WPF), fly ash (FA), and lime — to remediate desiccation-ridden embankment soil from Tahirpur, Sunamganj. The optimum blend delivers a 242.85% CBR increase, 150% UCS gain, 91.17% crack volume reduction, and 83.54% surface cracking reduction.</p>
<p><strong>Key areas:</strong> Waste plastic fiber, fly ash, lime stabilization, desiccation remediation, embankment soil</p>

<h3><a href="/research-projects/soil-stabilization-lime/" style="color: #52adc8; text-decoration: none;">Ternary Stabilisation of Silty Subsoil Using Lime, Rice Husk Ash, and Eggshell Powder</a></h3>
<p><strong>Status:</strong> Under review at <em>Journal of Material Cycles and Waste Management</em> &nbsp;|&nbsp; <strong>Duration:</strong> 2022 – 2024</p>
<p>A ternary blend of lime with two locally available agricultural wastes — rice husk ash (RHA) and eggshell powder (ESP) — as a stabiliser for silty subsoil. The optimum blend of 4% lime, 10% RHA, and 3% ESP reduced the plasticity index by 58.6% and raised unconfined compressive strength by 103.3%.</p>
<p><strong>Key areas:</strong> Lime stabilization, rice husk ash, eggshell powder, ternary soil stabilization, sustainable construction</p>
