<h3><a href="/research-projects/zeolite-cellulose/">Natural Zeolite and Cellulose Fiber Stabilization of Sunamganj Embankment Soil</a></h3>
<p><strong>Status:</strong> Ongoing, funded by CRISP &nbsp;|&nbsp; <strong>Role:</strong> Principal Investigator &nbsp;|&nbsp; <strong>Duration:</strong> July 2026 – June 2027</p>
<p>The flood embankments in Sunamganj crack and fail often. In this project I'm testing natural zeolite (5 to 20%) and then cellulose fiber (0.25 to 1%) as a cheaper, lower-carbon option than cement or lime. I'm using a drone survey to choose the sampling sites. Direct shear, UCS and SEM-EDS tests will give the best mix. Then PLAXIS 2D and a life cycle assessment will check if it works for a full embankment and how it compares on carbon. I'm the PI and I supervise two B.Sc. students.</p>
<p><strong>Key areas:</strong> Natural zeolite, cellulose fiber, two-stage stabilization, drone survey, PLAXIS 2D, life cycle assessment</p>
</div>

<h3><a href="/research-projects/desiccation-ml/">Gradient-Boosted and Symbolic Regression Modelling of Desiccation Cracking</a></h3>
<p><strong>Status:</strong> Ongoing &nbsp;|&nbsp; <strong>Duration:</strong> June 2026 – Present</p>
<p>My lab work showed how much each additive reduced cracking, but I didn't know which factors mattered most across different soils and climates. I collected desiccation test data from published studies and trained LightGBM, XGBoost and CatBoost models on it. I then used symbolic regression to turn the models into simple equations. One finding is that each crack measure depends on a different main factor.</p>
<p><strong>Key areas:</strong> Gradient boosting, symbolic regression, SHAP, desiccation cracking, embankment soil</p>
</div>

<h3><a href="/research-projects/slope-stability/">Physically Admissible Slope-Stability Prediction Using Bayesian Physics-Informed Neural Networks</a></h3>
<p><strong>Status:</strong> Manuscript in preparation &nbsp;|&nbsp; <strong>Duration:</strong> December 2025 – August 2026</p>
<p>Many machine learning models for slopes give answers that break soil mechanics. I built a Bayesian physics-informed network that can't do this, because the rules are built into the network itself. I pretrained it on 2,000 of 3,000 PLAXIS 2D runs that I automated with Python, then trained it on 275 real slope cases. It had zero rule violations. The standard models broke the rules in 19 to 31% of tests outside their training range. Pretraining also improved accuracy in all five cross-validation folds.</p>
<p><strong>Key areas:</strong> Physics-informed neural networks, Bayesian deep learning, slope stability, geotechnical classification</p>

<h3><a href="/research-projects/bio-cementation/">Assessment of Bio-cementation Capability of <em>Arthrospira platensis</em> in Sand Solidification</a></h3>
<p><strong>Status:</strong> First-author manuscript under review at <em>Geosystems and Geoenvironment</em> &nbsp;|&nbsp; <strong>Duration:</strong> 2023 – 2024</p>
<p>This was my undergraduate thesis. I treated Ottawa sand with <em>Arthrospira platensis</em> and a calcium chloride and urea solution at five concentrations. Then I measured permeability, shear strength and ultrasonic pulse velocity. Permeability dropped by 71.9% at 1.00 M, and shear strength was highest at 0.60 M. SEM-EDS confirmed that calcite was holding the grains together.</p>
<p><strong>Key areas:</strong> Bio-cementation, cyanobacteria-induced calcite precipitation, sustainable soil stabilization, MICP</p>
</div>

<h3><a href="/research-projects/desiccation-cracking/">Mitigating Desiccation-Induced Cracking of Embankment Soil Using Fiber Reinforcement and Fly Ash</a></h3>
<p><strong>Status:</strong> Published in <em>Geotechnical and Geological Engineering</em> (2025) &nbsp;|&nbsp; <strong>Duration:</strong> 2023 – 2024</p>
<p>We tested polypropylene fiber first and polyester fiber after that, with and without fly ash, on embankment soil that cracks as it dries. Polyester fiber with fly ash cut the surface crack ratio by up to 96.4% and raised UCS by 45%.</p>
<p><strong>Key areas:</strong> Desiccation cracking, fiber reinforcement, fly ash stabilization, embankment soil, crack mitigation</p>

<h3><a href="/research-projects/embankment-soil/">Remediation of Desiccation-Ridden Embankment Soil Using Waste Plastic Fiber, Fly Ash, and Lime</a></h3>
<p><strong>Status:</strong> Under review at <em>Indian Geotechnical Journal</em> &nbsp;|&nbsp; <strong>Duration:</strong> 2022 – 2024</p>
<p>We added waste plastic fiber, fly ash and lime in three stages to embankment soil from Tahirpur, Sunamganj. The best mix raised CBR by 242.85% and UCS by 150%. It also cut crack volume by 91.17% and surface cracking by 83.54%.</p>
<p><strong>Key areas:</strong> Waste plastic fiber, fly ash, lime stabilization, desiccation remediation, embankment soil</p>

<h3><a href="/research-projects/soil-stabilization-lime/">Ternary Stabilisation of Silty Subsoil Using Lime, Rice Husk Ash, and Eggshell Powder</a></h3>
<p><strong>Status:</strong> Under review at <em>Journal of Material Cycles and Waste Management</em> &nbsp;|&nbsp; <strong>Duration:</strong> 2022 – 2024</p>
<p>We mixed lime with two farm wastes, rice husk ash and eggshell powder, to improve a silty soil. The best mix was 4% lime, 10% rice husk ash and 3% eggshell powder. It lowered the plasticity index by 58.6% and doubled the UCS.</p>
<p><strong>Key areas:</strong> Lime stabilization, rice husk ash, eggshell powder, ternary soil stabilization, sustainable construction</p>
